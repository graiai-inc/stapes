## Abstract

Ambient AI clinical scribes rely on speech recognition, yet privacy-preserving on-device models have not been benchmarked against cloud services on medical conversation data, and the patient-safety implications of recognition errors in medication names have not been quantified across deployment modes. We benchmarked 14 on-device ASR models and 5 cloud APIs on 400 clinical conversations from three publicly available datasets (OSCE, primary care, psychiatry). On-device models matched cloud clinical term recall within 1–3 percentage points and word error rate within 0.5–4 percentage points of the best cloud service on each dataset. Drug names exhibited the highest error rates of any clinical term category across every model and every deployment mode, with individual medications such as wellbutrin misrecognized in over 60% of occurrences, including by cloud services marketed as medical-specific. ROVER fusion yielded ≤ 0.82 percentage point improvements, three-model combinations did not help, and six advanced fusion algorithms did not exceed naive equal-weight voting in a depth-study. Cloud costs ranged from $19 to $383 for the benchmark, compared with zero marginal cost on-device. Privacy-preserving on-device ASR is a viable alternative for clinical documentation, but neither deployment mode currently transcribes medication names reliably; clinical use requires explicit medication verification before notes are finalized, and single-model domain adaptation is more promising than multi-model fusion as a path to closing the remaining gap.
