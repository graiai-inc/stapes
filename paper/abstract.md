## Abstract

Clinical documentation consumes over half of physicians' working hours and is a leading driver of burnout. Ambient AI scribes depend on accurate speech recognition, yet the accuracy of privacy-preserving on-device models has not been benchmarked against commercial cloud services on medical conversation data. We evaluated 14 general-purpose on-device ASR models and 5 commercial cloud APIs across 400 clinical conversations from three publicly available datasets spanning OSCE examinations, primary care, and psychiatry. On-device models achieved clinical term recall within 1 to 3 percentage points of cloud APIs, and word error rate within 0.5 to 4 percentage points of the best cloud service on each dataset. Drug names exhibited the highest error rates across all models regardless of deployment mode. ROVER hypothesis fusion produced only modest improvements (≤ 0.82 percentage points) over the best single model, adding a third model did not help, and advanced fusion algorithms evaluated in a depth-study did not exceed naive equal-weight voting. Cloud costs for the benchmark ranged from $19 to $383; on-device inference required no per-encounter expenditure. On-device ASR is a viable alternative for privacy-preserving clinical documentation, with single-model domain adaptation a more promising direction than multi-model fusion.
