## Abstract

Ambient AI clinical scribes rely on speech recognition, yet privacy-preserving on-device models have not been benchmarked against cloud services on medical conversation data. We benchmarked 14 on-device ASR models and 5 cloud APIs on 400 clinical conversations from three publicly available datasets (OSCE, primary care, psychiatry). On-device models matched cloud clinical term recall within 1–3 percentage points and word error rate within 0.5–4 percentage points of the best cloud service on each dataset. Drug names had the highest error rates across all models regardless of deployment mode. ROVER fusion yielded ≤ 0.82 percentage point improvements, three-model combinations did not help, and six advanced fusion algorithms did not exceed naive voting in a depth-study. Cloud costs ranged from $19 to $383 for the benchmark, compared with zero marginal cost on-device. Privacy-preserving on-device ASR is a viable alternative for clinical documentation; single-model domain adaptation is more promising than multi-model fusion.
